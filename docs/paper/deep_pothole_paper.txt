Abstract—To address the passability issues of a corner
module architecture intelligent electric vehicle (CAV) on
deep-potholed roads during emergency rescue operations,
the motion posture control of the CAV is investigated. First,
the posture coordinated control ﬂow was designed, and
the stability mechanism of the vehicle supported by three
wheels is formulated. Second, a model for three-wheel sup-
ported vehicle (TWSV) is established, including the tran-
sition process between TWSV and four-wheel supported
vehicle. Third, a novel corner module was designed, inte-
grating in-wheel motor drive system, wheel-side steer-by-
wire system, brake by-wire system, and active suspension
system (ASS) into one unit. A coordinated control method
based on feedforward and feedback is proposed. The feed-
forward value is calculated based on the vertical load of
the TWSV, and the feedback controller is designed based
on Takagi–Sugeno robust sliding mode control. Finally, the
correctness of the ASS model with ball screw is veriﬁed
through experiments. The validity of the new dynamics is
conﬁrmed through comparison with CarSim, and the effec-
tiveness of the coordinated control strategy is validated
through hardware-in-the-loop testing. This provides a the-
oretical foundation for the passability control of CAV on
deep-potholed roads.
Index Terms—Active suspension systems (ASS), corner
module architecture, deep-potholed roads, intelligent elec-
tric vehicle, motion posture control.

I. INTRODUCTION
T
HE corner module architecture intelligent electric vehicle
(CAV) integrates in-wheel motor drive system, wheel-side
steer-by-wire system, brake by-wire system, and active suspen-
sion system (ASS) [1], greatly simplifying the vehicle layout and
improving drivetrain efﬁciency. The in-wheel motor drive sys-
temcanindependentlyadjustthetorqueandspeedofeachwheel,
enabling control of the vehicle dynamics performance [2], [3],
[4]. The wheel-side steer-by-wire system allows for omnidirec-
tional steering capabilities [5], [6], [7], [8], whereas the ASS
improve ride comfort and enable vehicle body posture control
[9], [10], [11]. Therefore, the CAV possesses handling, trafﬁc
ability, and stability that conventional vehicles cannot match.
It can effectively compensate for the negative effects, such as
degradation in ride comfort, increased costs, and difﬁculties
in modiﬁcation that arise from matching in-wheel motor drive
system. The CAV represents an ideal platform for studying op-
timal vehicle dynamics and holds signiﬁcant market prospects.
Especially in harsh operating conditions, the advantages of the
CAV will become even more apparent.
In the event of natural disasters, such as rain or snow, narrow
roads may collapse, leading to the formation of deep potholes.
In such situations, it becomes necessary to conduct rescue op-
erations through these roads. However, vehicles equipped with
passive suspension systems are unable to pass over due to the
depth of the potholes. The performance of vehicles equipped
with ASS in terms of passability in the aforementioned scenarios
has not been studied. However, achieving passability poses
signiﬁcant challenges due to inadequate integration with the
full vehicle dynamics control system. On the other hand, the
CAV, with its dynamics advantages, can relatively easily achieve
good posture control and passability in the mentioned scenarios.
However, research on the CAV is still in the exploratory stage.
The concept of the corner module architecture for chassis
systems emerged in the early 21st century. Volvo designed a pro-
totype named the Autonomous Corner Module in 2002 and 2008
[12], [13], but its revolutionary nature prevented it from mass
production. In 2006, Siemens VDO developed the e-Corner,
integratingtheentirechassissystemwithinthewheel.Bycoordi-
nating the control of each corner module, stable driving could be
achieved [14]. In 2019, Protean Electric introduced the Protean
360+ and Schaefﬂer developed the e-Corner, both providing

facilitates the smooth and efﬁcient transportation of passengers
and goods in busy urban areas.
In terms of theoretical research, a corner module system for
urban vehicles was designed [16], and its geometric shape was
optimized using a genetic algorithm. A corner module utilizing a
double four-bar linkage mechanism to generate a virtual kingpin
axis was designed [17]. A four-wheel independent drive and

However, the primary objectives of these developments are to
achieve chassis integration and improve vehicle maneuverability

pension deﬂection (SD), resulting in insufﬁcient passability,
making them unsuitable for use on damaged structured roads
and unstructured roads. This disadvantage becomes particularly
prominent,especiallyinharshroadconditionsduringemergency
rescue operations.
ASS play a pivotal role in adjusting the vehicle’s posture,
and both domestic and international experts have extensively
researched this aspect. Although a chassis control system was
designed to ensure stability during high-speed turns [21], un-
fortunately, no real vehicle experiments were conducted. A
sliding mode control strategy, based on a state observer, was
developed to manage vehicle posture and ensure stability during
lane-keeping [22]. However, this strategy overlooked the impact
of wheel slip on the overall control approach. The utilization of
model predictive control (MPC) for posture control has been
explored [23], [24], but its practical application may pose chal-
lengesduetohighhardwarerequirements.Acoordinatedcontrol
method was proposed to address the roll control issue in vehicles
with mechanical elastic wheels, effectively adjusting the posture
[25]. It is evident that the aforementioned studies primarily focus
on posture control on ﬂat road surfaces, and their applicability
to the extremely harsh road conditions addressed in this article
is not suitable.
Theresearchonposturecontrolinchallengingroadconditions
can provide valuable insights for the work presented in this
article. The investigation into roll stability on sloping roads led
to the development of a priority MPC [26]. However, it assumes
that the slope angle remains constant, which does not align
well with real-world conditions. Addressing the critical issue
of tripping rollovers, a new rollover criterion was introduced,
and robust control, optimized through genetic algorithms, was
implemented [27]. Yet, the control of wheel slip was not ef-
fectively managed. In the pursuit of enhancing off-road capa-
bility on uneven road surfaces, the studied approach deviates
signiﬁcantly from CAV [28], [29], raising questions about its
applicabilitytoCAV.Thedynamiccouplingrelationshipsamong
in-wheel motors, suspension systems, steering systems, and the
lateral motion of the vehicle in both longitudinal and lateral
planes were derived [30], [31]. While this signiﬁcantly enhances
lateral stability on uneven road surfaces, ASS was not taken
into consideration. An explicit MPC-based method for vehicle
body posture control was designed [32], focusing on a 1/4
ASS. However, the applicability of the algorithm to the full
vehicle with a high-coupling ASS system might be questionable.
Posture adjustment based on a slow ASS has been developed
[33], but the adjustment speed of the suspension is relatively
slow. The above studies, however, do not speciﬁcally address
the characteristics of CAV and their motion posture adjustment.
In addition, the mentioned research is not applicable to CAV
in such road conditions. There is a gap in research regarding
the enhancement of drivability on deep-potholed roads through
motion posture adjustment, especially in the context of emer-
gency rescue operations. This is a critical issue for ensuring the
people’s safety.
This article focuses on CAV and proposes the ASS coordi-
nated control method to pass over roads with deep potholes.
The main contributions of this article are as follows.
1) A coordinated control process for ASS is designed to
enable the CAV to navigate through deep-potholed roads,
and a dynamic mechanism for ensuring the safe motion
posture of the vehicle based on three-wheel support is
established.
2) A model for three-wheel supported vehicle (TWSV)
is established, including the transition process between
TWSV and four-wheel supported vehicle (FWSV).
3) Feedforward controller based on the vertical load of the
TWSV and feedback controller based on Takagi–Sugeno
robust sliding mode control (T-SRSMC) are designed,
and the stability of the feedback controller with the feed-
forward value is proven.
4) The accuracy of the constructed model was validated by
CarSim, and the correctness of the overall vehicle control
method was veriﬁed through hardware-in-the-loop (HIL)
testing, with a maximum achievable speed of 12.8 km/h.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Section II,
the coordinated control process is designed, and the optimal
stability mechanism is analyzed. In Section III, the designed
corner module structure is introduced, and a model of a vehicle
supported by three wheels and the ASS using a ball screw are
built. The coordinated controller is designed in Section IV. The
experiment is conducted on the ASS with a ball screw, and HIL
testing is performed in Section V. Finally, Section VI concludes
this article.

ordinated controller consists of Steps 0–4. Steps 1 and 3 are
composed of feedback control u and feedforward control uf.

technology, and a feedback controller based on T-SRSMC is
designed to effectively enhance the accuracy and robustness of
the control. The SD is set using optimal stability theory, and
the feedforward control force is calculated. The total suspension
control force us1 (the forces in Step 1) and us3 (the forces in Step
3) is obtained by adding the feedforward and feedback control
forces. We refer to Steps 2 and 4 as transitional steps. In these
two steps, the forces are gradually reduced to 0 within a certain

VI. CONCLUSION
Based on the excellent dynamics performance of the CAV,
this article investigates the issue of the posture control and pass-
ability on deep-potholed roads. The mechanism of the vehicle
supported by three wheels is analyzed, and a dynamic model for
TWSV is established, effectively expanding traditional dynamic
models. Controller design considers the changes in stable states
and sprung mass, enhancing control precision and robustness.
The validation conﬁrms that the constructed dynamic model and
the full vehicle control strategy are correct. With a pothole length
of 0.8 m, the achievable maximum traversal speed is 12.8 km/h.
This lays a theoretical foundation for the further development
of CAV.
